Spinodal amplification of baryon-density fluctuations: first reproduction of the fluid-dynamical results

Status report, 23 November 2026. Code: github.com/matplo/spinodal-fluid (public, BSD-3-Clause).

Summary. We re-implemented the fluid-dynamical model of Steinheimer & Randrup (PRL 109, 212301; PRC 87, 054903) in a new C++/Kokkos code that runs on CPUs and GPUs, with a finite-temperature two-phase equation of state, initial states from the SMASH hadron cascade, and the papers' observables. A first energy scan (central Pb+Pb, Elab = 1–6 A GeV, 50 events per energy, grid spacing 0.2 fm as in the papers) reproduces the central result: with a first-order phase transition the high-order density moments are enhanced by an order of magnitude at intermediate energies (largest at 2–3 A GeV), while the Maxwell partner equation of state, which has no spinodal instability, gives only a small enhancement.

1. Physics question

If the QCD phase diagram has a first-order transition at high baryon density, matter compressed in a heavy-ion collision may pass through the mechanically unstable (spinodal) region of that transition. Density fluctuations then grow exponentially and the baryon density becomes clumpy, which shows up in high-order normalised density moments ⟨ρN⟩/⟨ρ⟩N. The papers computed this in ideal fluid dynamics with a gradient (finite-range) term that sets the length scale of the clumps; the comparison is between a two-phase equation of state and its Maxwell partner, which has the same thermodynamics outside the coexistence region but no instability.

2. What was built

  • Fluid solver: 3+1D ideal relativistic fluid dynamics with the SHASTA flux-corrected transport algorithm (as in the papers), the gradient pressure p = p0(ε,ρ) − a²(εs/ρs²) ρ∇²ρ with a = 0.033 fm, outflow boundaries. One code base runs serially, with OpenMP and on NVIDIA GPUs (Kokkos); CPU and GPU results agree to rounding level. On one NVIDIA TITAN RTX an event takes 22–31 s, about 3× faster than 16 CPU threads.
  • Equation of state: hadron gas with a nuclear mean field and a bag-model plasma joined by a quintic spline as in Randrup, PRC 82, 034902. The papers do not specify the finite-temperature construction; we built one that is thermodynamically consistent and stable by construction (positive heat capacity everywhere, a crossover at small density) with the critical point at Tc = 142.5 MeV, and its Maxwell partner. Both are tabulated on an (ε, ρ) lattice.
  • Initial state: SMASH 3.4.2 cascade events for central Pb+Pb, each hadron deposited as a Gaussian of width 1 fm at the papers' start time.
  • Observables: normalised moments N = 2…7 versus time, per-event maximum enhancement, clump sizes, transverse flow.
  • Validation: analytic shock tubes and vacuum expansion; spinodal growth rates against the linear dispersion relation (within 1–5% at 0.1 fm, about 14% low at 0.2 fm); interface tension; the linear-response formulas of Randrup (2010); conservation and zero solver failures in all collision runs. Every run parameter (grid spacing, box, equation of state, a, initial-state width, energy) is one configuration key; instructions are in docs/running_collisions.md.

3. Equation of state

Phase diagram of the two-phase equation of state (blue: coexistence; orange: isothermal spinodal; green: isentropic spinodal, which drives the instability in an expanding fluid) compared with the curves of arXiv:1007.1448 Fig. 5 (black). The critical point is at 142.5 MeV as in the paper, at 2.93 ρs (paper 2.78 ρs). Our isentropic spinodal closes at about 112 MeV, lower than in the paper (about 127 MeV).

5. What agrees and what does not yet

  • Agrees: the rise and fall of the two-phase enhancement with beam energy and its magnitude (at 3–5 A GeV within 20% of the paper for N = 7, within 5% at 3–5 A GeV for N = 5); a small Maxwell enhancement at low and intermediate energy; the duration of the fluid stage at 3 A GeV (about 3.5 fm/c).
  • Peak position: our largest value is at 2 A GeV, the paper's at 3 A GeV; with 50 events the two are not resolved (difference 4.9 ± 3.8).
  • Maxwell at high energy: our Maxwell enhancement rises to about 2 at 5–6 A GeV. The two papers differ from each other here (1209.2462: up to 1.7, 1302.2956: about 1.2). A likely cause is the initial state: our SMASH initial states are more clumpy than the papers' UrQMD ones (N = 7 moment 93 vs 59 at the start of the fluid stage at 3 A GeV).
  • Start time: the result is sensitive to when the fluid stage starts. With tinit = 2R/√(γ²−1) and R = 6.62 fm (used here) the N = 7 two-phase enhancement at 3 A GeV is 11.5 ± 2.3 (20 events); with R = 1.2 A1/3 fm = 7.11 fm it is 3.4 ± 0.9.
  • Grid resolution: the papers use 0.2 fm. At 0.1 fm the two-phase N = 7 maximum rises by a factor 1.2–2.1 (5 events at 3 A GeV; mean 14.1 → 24.9), while Maxwell barely changes, consistent with the growth rates being underestimated at 0.2 fm. The amplification is therefore not converged at the papers' grid.
  • Timing: our N = 7 maximum occurs about 1.9 fm/c after the start at 3 A GeV, the paper's about 3 fm/c.
  • Equation of state: the finite-temperature construction is ours (not specified in the papers); its isentropic spinodal ends at a lower temperature than the paper's.

6. Next steps (running)

  1. Extend the scan to 200 events per energy (paper statistics), to locate the peak and settle the Maxwell trend.
  2. Resolution study at 3 A GeV with 0.1 fm grid spacing and 20 events per equation of state.
  3. Initial-state study at 3 A GeV: Gaussian widths 0.7 and 0.5 fm and the Lorentz-contraction convention of the deposition, to quantify how much of the difference to the papers comes from the initial state.
