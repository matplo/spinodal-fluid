Spinodal amplification: energy scan with 200 events per energy

Status report, 24 November 2026. Follows the pilot scan and the resolution and initial-state studies. Code: github.com/matplo/spinodal-fluid.

Summary. With the papers' statistics (200 events per energy and equation of state, central Pb+Pb, Elab = 1–6 A GeV, grid 0.2 fm, finite-temperature equation of state, start time with R = 6.62 fm), the two-phase equation of state gives a clear maximum of the density-moment enhancement at intermediate energies (N = 7: 17.4 ± 2.0 at 2 A GeV, 13.1 ± 1.2 at 3 A GeV), an order of magnitude above its Maxwell partner. The maximum lies at 2 A GeV in our calculation and at 3 A GeV in the papers. The Maxwell enhancement rises slowly with beam energy, more than in the papers. 2400 events, 0 solver failures.

2. Position of the maximum and the Maxwell trend

  • Position. On the 1 A GeV grid the two-phase maximum is at 2 A GeV with bootstrap probability 0.97 (N = 7) and 0.83 (N = 5); the difference between 2 and 3 A GeV is 4.4 ± 2.3 (N = 7) and 0.3 ± 0.3 (N = 5). A parabola through the three highest points peaks at 2.05 A GeV (N = 7, 68% interval 1.96–2.19) and 2.20 A GeV (N = 5). The papers peak at 3 A GeV. Above 3 A GeV our two-phase curve follows the papers closely (4 and 5 A GeV within about 10–15%).
  • Maxwell. Our Maxwell enhancement rises with energy (N = 7: from 1.06 at 1 A GeV to 2.08 at 6 A GeV, slope 0.23 ± 0.02 per A GeV), so the two-phase/Maxwell ratio falls from about 10 at 2 A GeV to 1.2 at 6 A GeV. The papers' Maxwell curves are flatter and differ from each other (1209.2462: up to 1.7; 1302.2956: up to 1.2).

The two-phase versus Maxwell difference survives every variation except the narrowest initial Gaussians, but its size changes by factors of 1.5–3 with choices that the papers fix by convention. The largest single effect is the start time of the fluid stage. The grid effect means that 0.2 fm results underestimate the model's continuum amplification; whether this also moves the position of the maximum is not yet measured.

4. Interpretation and next steps

  • The qualitative result of the papers is reproduced with an independent code, a different cascade (SMASH instead of UrQMD) and our own finite-temperature construction of the equation of state: a first-order phase transition produces a strong, non-monotonic enhancement of high-order baryon-density moments versus beam energy, which its Maxwell partner does not.
  • The shift of the maximum from 3 to 2 A GeV and the rising Maxwell baseline are most likely tied to the initial state (our SMASH initial state is more clumpy than the papers' UrQMD one) and to the equation of state above 110 MeV (our isentropic spinodal ends at about 112 MeV, the paper's at about 127 MeV).
  • Candidate next steps: the energy scan at dx = 0.1 fm for 2 and 3 A GeV (does the maximum move?); calibrating the initial state to the papers' initial moments; varying the equation-of-state construction above 110 MeV; dissipative and fluctuating fluid dynamics (planned extensions).
