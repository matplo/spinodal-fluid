Particlisation: from the fluid to particles

Status report, 25 November 2026. Follows the energy scan. Code: github.com/matplo/spinodal-fluid (pull request #23).

Summary. The fluid code now produces final-state particles. A four-dimensional switching surface at constant energy density (εsw = 4 εs) is found on the GPU during the run. A Cooper–Frye sampler then emits nucleons, antinucleons and pions with baryon number and electric charge conserved exactly in every sample. At 3 A GeV almost the whole switching surface lies inside the coexistence (spline) region of the two-phase equation of state, so the sampler's chemical potential is now fixed by the local baryon density (decision S24). In the first end-to-end test about two thirds of the particles come from matter that is already below the switching density when the fluid stage starts; a switchable core–corona treatment is in preparation. These particles are the input for the planned classifier study of whether a first-order phase transition is detectable in momentum space.

1. Why particlisation

The density moments of the earlier reports are computed from the fluid's baryon density in coordinate space, which no experiment observes. To ask whether the spinodal clumping survives into measurable momentum-space distributions (point clouds px, py, pz, species per event) the fluid has to be converted to particles. A previous study with the same model (arXiv:1906.06562) found 95% per-event separation of two-phase and Maxwell events in coordinate space, but only 52–54% in momentum space. The chain implemented here is: switching surface → Cooper–Frye sampling → (next) SMASH hadronic afterburner.

2. Switching surface

  • Method. Iso-energy surface ε = εsw in (t, x, y, z), found cell by cell on the GPU with a Cornelius-type algorithm (Huovinen & Petersen, arXiv:1206.3371). Default εsw = 4 εs (as in arXiv:1906.06562), variant 5 εs; a constant-time surface (as in arXiv:1311.0999) is available as a cross-check. Values at the surface are interpolated from the conserved fields.
  • Initial slice. Cells already below εsw at the start time tinit are emitted at once (decision S23).
  • Tests. Closed analytic surfaces sum to zero (|Σ dΣ| / Σ |dΣ| ≤ 7e-17); sphere area within 0.16%; moving slab exact to 1e-14; Hubble expansion position within 5e-4. Serial, OpenMP and CUDA agree.
  • Energy closure. The energy crossing the surface misses the initial energy by 1.3% at dx = 0.2 fm in the test case and by 0.65% in collision events; it converges with the grid (3.4%, 1.3%, 0.49% at dx = 0.4, 0.2, 0.1 fm). The target of 1e-3 is not reached at the production grid; the deficit is the same for both equations of state.
  • Cost. About 1.2 million surface elements per event (150 MB). On the TITAN RTX: fluid 8 s + surface 2 s per event at 3 A GeV. Production will sample particles in place and not store surfaces.

Where the switching surface lies in the (ρ, T) plane, weighted by the energy flux, for 20 events at 3 A GeV: two-phase (left) and Maxwell (right) equation of state, εsw = 4 εs (top) and 5 εs (bottom). Solid line a(T), dashed line b(T): the hadron-side and plasma-side matching densities of the two-phase construction; between them is the spline (coexistence) region. 97% (4 εs) and 100% (5 εs) of the flux lie inside it, at ρ ≈ 2–3.5 ρs and T ≈ 100–142 MeV.

3. Particle sampler

  • Species. Default: nucleons and antinucleons (Fermi statistics) and pions (Bose), the degrees of freedom of the fluid's hadronic phase; a full hadron-resonance gas is available as a variant.
  • Chemical potential (decision S24). Because the surface lies in the coexistence region, where the equation of state contains a plasma admixture, the hadronic chemical potential of the table does not reproduce the fluid's baryon density (up to 67% off at T = 136–142 MeV). The default now takes T from the table and solves, element by element, for the nucleon chemical potential that reproduces the local baryon density (residual ≤ 1e-12 in the relevant range). The energy then cannot also be matched: the mean-field energy of the fluid has no particle counterpart, and the particles carry 94.6% of the surface energy (96.4% with the table rule).
  • Exact conservation. Baryon number and charge are conserved exactly in every sample by conditioning the Poisson emission on (B, Q), without rejection; the result was checked against brute-force rejection on a small system (multiplicities and spectra, p ≥ 0.09). A simpler shortcut (fixing the nucleon numbers, then drawing charges) was tested and found biased (p = 1e-4), and was not used.
  • Output. OSCAR2013 particle lists, ready for the SMASH afterburner.

5. Finding: the corona dominates the particle count

About two thirds of the sampled particles come from the initial slice: matter that is already below the switching density at tinit and never passes through the fluid evolution. These particles are identical for both equations of state and therefore dilute any signal. Four treatments are being implemented as one switch, so that their effect can be measured: core–corona (default; the cascade particles of those regions are passed on unchanged, as in hybrid models), thermal (the present emission), keep (the cells stay in the fluid and are emitted at the end), and a lower switching density (2–3 εs).

6. Next steps

  • Corona switch and comparison of the four treatments.
  • SMASH as hadronic afterburner (List modus).
  • Dataset pipeline: paired events (the same initial state for every equation of state), point clouds for the classifier.
  • A crossover equation of state built from the same pure phases.
  • Classifier: first reproduce the published momentum-space result, then test the effect of each modelling choice.
