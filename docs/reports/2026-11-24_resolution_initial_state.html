Spinodal amplification: grid-resolution and initial-state studies at 3 A GeV

Status report, 24 November 2026. Follows the first energy scan. Code: github.com/matplo/spinodal-fluid.

Summary. Two systematic studies of the 3 A GeV result (central Pb+Pb, finite-temperature equation of state, start time with R = 6.62 fm). (1) Halving the grid spacing from 0.2 fm (the papers' value) to 0.1 fm raises the two-phase N = 7 enhancement by a factor 1.8 on average, while the Maxwell result barely changes: the spinodal amplification is not converged at the papers' grid. (2) The width and Lorentz contraction of the Gaussians with which the cascade hadrons are deposited change the result as much as the grid does: narrower Gaussians make the initial state more clumpy, raise the Maxwell enhancement and reduce the two-phase/Maxwell contrast. No solver failures in any of the 440 events.

1. Grid resolution

The same 20 SMASH events were evolved with dx = 0.2 fm and dx = 0.1 fm in the same physical box (190×190×170 and 380×380×340 cells), for both equations of state. The per-event comparison removes the event-to-event spread.

The two-phase/Maxwell ratio of the N = 7 means grows from 8.3 to 12.7. This matches the growth-rate tests of the solver: at 0.2 fm the fastest spinodal growth rate is about 14% below the analytic value and the shortest unstable wavelengths are cut off, while 0.1 fm reproduces the dispersion relation within a few percent. Because the amplification is exponential in the growth rate, a 14% rate deficit becomes a large difference in the moments. The papers' 0.2 fm results are therefore probably below the continuum value of their own model. Cost: 0.2 fm about 24 s per event, 0.1 fm about 5.5 min per event and 14 GB on the GPU.

2. Initial state

Each hadron from the cascade is deposited as a Gaussian. The papers use a width σ = 1 fm (variants 0.7 and 0.5 fm) contracted along the beam by a γ factor that they do not define precisely; our default contracts each Gaussian with the hadron's own γ. We ran the pilot's 50 events with three variants.

  • Width. Narrower Gaussians make the initial density more clumpy (raw N = 7 moment 90 → 189 → 560 for σ = 1.0, 0.7, 0.5 fm). The enhancement is measured relative to the initial value, so a clumpy start leaves less room to grow and the expansion smooths it: at σ = 0.7 fm the Maxwell enhancement rises to about 4 and the two-phase/Maxwell ratio falls from 8.8 to 3.5; at σ = 0.5 fm almost no enhancement remains for either equation of state.
  • Contraction. Contracting all Gaussians with the centre-of-mass γ instead of each hadron's own γ raises the two-phase N = 7 enhancement from 12.5 to 18.8 and the Maxwell one from 1.4 to 2.5.
  • Consequence. The initial state is a systematic uncertainty of the same size as the grid resolution. Our SMASH initial state at σ = 1 fm is already more clumpy than the papers' UrQMD one (90 vs about 59), which is consistent with our larger Maxwell enhancement at high energy in the first scan.

3. What this means for the comparison with the papers

The two-phase versus Maxwell signal is robust: it is present for every grid and every initial-state variant except the narrowest Gaussians. Its size, however, depends on choices that the papers fix by convention (grid 0.2 fm, σ = 1 fm, contraction): factors of about 1.5–2 in the N = 7 enhancement. A quantitative comparison with the published numbers is therefore meaningful only at the papers' settings, and the physics conclusions should be stated together with these systematic uncertainties.
